# Customer validation kit · business banking choice · 2026-10-06

Goal: identify who owns the bank-selection decision and whether the prototype helps them choose an explainable next step. The current hypothesis is a founder/operator of a Portuguese Lda. Collect five recent concrete cases and two usability walkthroughs. Do not collect bank documents, IDs or confidential account data.

## Interview · 20 minutes

1. Describe the last business account choice: entity, country, date, trigger and operations it needed to support.
2. Which banks/products were considered and ruled out? What evidence influenced the decision? Separate official conditions, personal experience, reviews and assumptions.
3. Which needs were essential: currencies held, international transfers, Portuguese tax/reference payments, team cards, cash, integrations or branch support? Which were preferences?
4. How did you compare subscriptions, taxes, FX and transaction charges? What was unknown until after opening?
5. What happened during onboarding: wrong channel, additional documents, delays or rejection? Who prepared the company information?
6. What tools/helpers did you use, and what did they cost? What was still difficult?
7. Show the synthetic prototype. Ask the person to choose needs, explain why an option is relevant, find an uncertainty, distinguish known subtotal from full cost, select a route and save the comparison.
8. Would the resulting next action have helped with that actual case? What is missing or misleading? What concrete upcoming task could use this, and what existing effort/budget would it replace?

## Usability acceptance record

Pseudonym/role; task and synthetic profile; observed actions; assistance required; misunderstood labels; essential-vs-preference interpretation; cost/annual-payment interpretation; eligibility-vs-readiness interpretation; chosen next step and supporting source; overall pass/revise verdict. Record observations, not hypothetical praise.

## Product decision after five cases

Select founder or adviser based on observed ownership and repeated decisions. Record counterexamples. If selection is easy but preparation is painful, revisit the priority; if neither is important, revisit the premise. Do not extrapolate demand or measured savings from hypothetical interest.

Recruitment and sending interview invitations require explicit owner instructions. No one has been contacted.
